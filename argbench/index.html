How well do large language models handle computational argumentation? ArgBench evaluates LLMs on 46 tasks from 33 datasets, grouped into five skills: mining arguments, assessing their perspective and quality, reasoning about them, and generating them.

Prompting

Models are prompted with each task's definition, without fine-tuning. Skill scores average the tasks of a skill; Macro averages the five skills. Click a column to sort.

Per-task results

Skills

    Evaluation

    • Classification: macro F1.
    • Segmentation: F1 of exact span matches, macro-averaged over positive span labels.
    • Generation: BERTScore F1.
    • Unparseable classification outputs receive a random candidate label.
    • Test sets are capped at 1,000 instances per task.

    Prompting setups

    • Zero-shot: task definition followed by the instance.
    • Few-shot: four training examples added after the definition.
    • Chain-of-thought: “Think step by step and prepend your output with Output:”.
    • Greedy decoding with the paper's per-task-type token limits, except chain-of-thought, which gets at least 8,192 tokens: with the paper's limits, most reasoning models were cut off before giving an answer.
